## Notes

**Champions: Kinshū**, the fourth-best qualifier, through the lower bracket. Hōrai beat them 3-1 in the group phase and Varahmehr beat them 2-1 in the upper semi-final. Kinshū then won four straight: Shivon, Divia 6-0, Varahmehr 1-0, and Hōrai 2-1 in the grand final.

**Varahmehr won all seven group matches by an aggregate of 30-6 and did not reach the grand final.** They lost the upper final 2-3 to Hōrai and the lower final 0-1 to Kinshū.

**Hōrai and Kinshū finished level on 18 points in Group A**, separated by goal difference, and met again in the grand final. Kinshū reversed the group result.

**Both lower-bracket first-round ties were rematches of the upper quarter-finals, and the same side won both.** Shivon lost to Kinshū twice, Selmira to Divia twice.

Sankt Gerold Island lost all seven, scoring once and conceding 44, including 0-9 to Varahmehr and 1-12 to South Pelagonia.

Nichirin's three PFA colonies finished 1st (Kinshū), 2nd (Hōrai) and 15th (Ryūgu, 7th in Group B).

## Continuity flags

- **Bracket seeding.** The upper semi-finals paired each group winner with a qualifier from the other group. The 1934 EUFA Championship bracket the same season did not cross, pairing each group winner with a qualifier from their own group. The two competitions used the same stated format.
- **Field unchanged from 1933.** The same 16 teams entered.
- **A West Bangala player named "E. Islam" survives in two archived files.** Avium renames its faiths (Kaiseido, Hasmonean, Shushtari, Nazarene, Gautamadharma), so a surname that is a real-world religion sits oddly beside them. The current preset no longer carries the name; it remains in 1933 WC PFA-VAFC Qualifier Player Stats and in the leaderboard in 1934 WC PFA Qualifier. Nothing to fix in this file, and whether Avium has an unnamed Islam analogue is an ACU question rather than a Nichirin one.
